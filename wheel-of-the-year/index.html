Wheel of the Year

Solo practices for the turning points of the sun.

The moon gives a monthly rhythm; the sun gives a yearly one. The modern pagan Wheel of the Year marks eight points on that circle: the two solstices, the two equinoxes, and the four "cross quarter" days between them. Some of those days have long histories in the British and Irish calendar. Others, including both equinoxes, have little evidence of ancient celebration, and the eight part wheel as a whole was assembled within modern witchcraft in the twentieth century (Hutton, The Stations of the Sun). Each practice here says which parts are inherited and which are invented. Only the practitioner decides what any of it means.

Fall Equinox: Balance Point

A solo practice for the September equinox: grounding on the east to west line, weighing the year's harvest, sending what is finished underground, opening to change, and channelling gathered energy toward it.

About September 21 to 24 · sunset facing west · about 45 minutes
