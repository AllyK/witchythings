Moon Majic

Working with the lunar cycle, one turning at a time.

The moon offers a rhythm you do not have to invent: roughly 29.5 days from new to full and back again, with an eclipse arriving a few times a year as a scheduled interruption. The practices in this portal borrow that timing. The new moon, when the sky is darkest, is used for beginnings and intentions. The full moon, when the light peaks, is used for noticing what has come to fullness and for putting things down. The eclipse ritual sits apart, built for the rarer moments when the ordinary order breaks and repairs itself.

As the moon moves through its cycle it also passes through each zodiac sign for about two to two and a half days at a time, which is why every new and full moon happens in a particular sign. Almanacs have tracked the moon's astrological place for centuries, mostly for planting and harvest timing. You can look up the current moon sign at Almanac.com or the Farmers' Almanac, and exact phase times for your location at timeanddate.com.

What is inherited, what is invented

Honesty about provenance, in the same spirit as the eclipse page. Timing practices by the moon is genuinely old; almanac traditions of moon-sign timing go back centuries. The specific pairing of new moon with intention setting and full moon with release, as practiced here, is largely contemporary, carried by modern witchcraft, pagan, and wellness communities rather than by any single ancient rite (see The Pagan Grimoire on the modern practice, and We'Moon for one lineage of it). The sign correspondences are traditional Western astrology, a symbolic system, not an empirical claim. Treat all of it as a framework for attention. Only the practitioner decides what it means.
